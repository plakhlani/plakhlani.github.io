The quote goes out. Then nothing. No yes, no no, just silence. After a few days, most owners assume the job is lost and move on.

Here is a harder question to sit with. How many of those quotes were truly lost on price, and how many were simply forgotten? If your follow-up system is your memory, the honest answer is that you do not know.

## The silent pipeline
Every manufacturing business has a pipeline of open quotes, whether or not anyone calls it that. Each quote is a chance of future work. Yet in many small shops, that pipeline is invisible. There is no list, no dates and no owner.

The owner sends a quote, goes back to the floor, and hopes. Follow-up happens on the drive home, if at all. The bigger the shop gets, the worse this becomes, because there are more quotes and fewer moments to think about them.

## The proof is in your inbox
You can test this today. Look at your sent folder from the last month and find the quotes you sent. Next to each one, ask: did I follow up? Did the buyer reply? Do I know the outcome?
Most owners find a handful of quotes with no outcome at all. They are not won and not lost. They simply vanished. Each one represents a job that may have been available with a single well-timed call.

## Why silence is not always a no
When a buyer does not reply, it can mean several things.
- They have not decided yet, and your quote sits in a queue.
- They were interrupted and forgot to reply.
- The project was delayed or paused.
- They are waiting for other quotes to arrive.
- They chose someone else and did not bother to tell you.
Without a follow-up, you cannot tell which it is. You treat all of them as a no, which means you throw away the ones that were a maybe.

## The cost of relying on memory
Memory fails in predictable ways. It favours recent, urgent things over quiet, important ones. A quote sent three weeks ago competes with a broken machine today, and the machine wins.
Memory also does not scale. At ten open quotes, you may keep track. At forty, you will not. And if the person who holds the list in their head is away, nobody else knows what is outstanding.

## What a better system looks like
A good follow-up system does not need to be complicated. It needs three things.
- A single list of every open quote.
- A follow-up date on each one.
- A routine for reviewing the list, such as ten minutes each Monday.
That is it. A spreadsheet or a whiteboard will do. The point is to move follow-up out of your head and into something that does not forget.

## Questions worth asking
- How many open quotes do I have right now?
- When did I last chase them?
- What is the total value sitting in limbo?
- If I were away for a week, who would follow up?
- How many of my lost quotes did I actually confirm were lost?
If you cannot answer the first question, that is the problem. A shop that cannot see its open quotes cannot manage them.

## Follow-up is free revenue
Winning new customers is expensive. Following up on a quote you have already written is cheap. The work is done, the price is set, and the buyer already knows who you are. A short call costs a few minutes.

Even if only a few of those quotes turn into jobs, the return is high compared with the effort. That is why I call follow-up free revenue. It does not need advertising or new tools, only a habit.

## Not nagging, helping
Some owners worry that following up makes them seem pushy. In practice, a polite check-in is usually welcome. Buyers are juggling many suppliers and appreciate a reminder from someone who is organised. The tone matters. Be helpful, ask if they need anything changed, and make it easy for them to reply.

## Next step
## A fair way to close dead quotes
Not every quote should stay open forever. After two or three polite follow-ups with no response, send a closing note: let the buyer know you are closing the quote, and that they are welcome to contact you if the project returns. This keeps the relationship friendly and clears your list, so the quotes that remain are the ones that genuinely deserve attention.

If quotes die in silence in your shop, start with the list. Then, if you want something that reminds you automatically and shows every open quote at a glance, a custom system built around your existing process can do it without disrupting production.
