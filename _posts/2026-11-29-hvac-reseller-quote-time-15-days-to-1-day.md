Quoting speed is one of the clearest levers a supply or manufacturing business has. This case study looks at an Australian HVAC reseller whose quotes were taking 15 days, and what changed when the process was redesigned. The client is kept anonymous.

The result, in one line: quote time fell from 15 days to 1 day.

## The starting point
The business sold HVAC products and needed to give customers accurate quotes. Each quote took around 15 days to produce. In a market where buyers often ask several suppliers for prices, that is a long time. A buyer who needs an answer this month is unlikely to wait two weeks.

Long quote times in businesses like this usually have a familiar cause. Information is spread across emails, spreadsheets, supplier price lists and people's heads. Each quote requires someone to gather it, check it, and put it together. Nothing is broken in a dramatic way. It is just slow.

## What was wrong with the old approach
I will describe the common pattern rather than invent details about this client's internal files. In slow quoting processes, several problems tend to combine.
- Pricing data lives in several places, so each quote starts with a search.
- The steps are manual, so one delay holds up everything behind it.
- There is no single view of open quotes, so follow-up depends on memory.
- Only one or two people know how to price certain items, which creates a bottleneck.
Any one of these slows a quote. Together they can turn a simple request into a two-week job.

## The approach: build around the existing process
Rather than asking the business to adopt an off-the-shelf tool and change how it worked, I built a custom quoting system around the way the team already operated. This matters. Systems fail when they ask people to abandon familiar habits overnight. A system built around the current process is easier to adopt, because it feels like the same work done with less friction.

The goal was clear and narrow: reduce the time from enquiry to a sent quote. No unrelated features, no long IT project. One problem, solved properly.

## The result
Quote time dropped from 15 days to 1 day.

That is the headline number, and it is a real one. It means a customer who sends an enquiry on Monday can receive a quote on Tuesday, rather than at the end of the following week. In a market where the first reply often sets the price expectation, that change can make a significant difference to which quotes turn into orders.

I have not published further figures, such as win-rate changes or revenue, because I want to share only results I can stand behind. If those numbers are added later, this article can be updated.

## Why the result matters beyond speed
A faster quote has knock-on effects.
- More enquiries answered. When quoting is quick, fewer enquiries are dropped because they were too hard to get to.
- Less owner time. The owner or senior staff are no longer the bottleneck for every quote.
- Better follow-up. A visible list of open quotes makes it easier to chase them.
- More confidence. The team can reply knowing the information is current and consistent.

## Lessons for other manufacturers
The lessons apply to a machine shop or fabricator as much as to a reseller.
1. Speed is usually a systems problem. Skilled people are not the issue. The path from information to a quote is.
2. Fix one problem at a time. A focused change delivers results sooner than a full overhaul.
3. Build around how people already work. Adoption is easier when the system respects the existing process.
4. Measure the before and after. Knowing it was 15 days made the improvement to 1 day visible and credible.

## What you can do now

### Questions to ask about your own quoting
You can use this case as a checklist for your own business. How long does a quote take today, from enquiry to reply? Where does the time go: waiting for people, waiting for data, or building from scratch? Who can send a quote if the owner is away? Could a repeat enquiry be answered the same day if the information were in one place? Honest answers to these four questions usually point straight at the first thing to fix.

## Frequently asked questions
**Would this work for a machine shop or fabricator?**
The principle applies to any business where quotes depend on scattered information and manual steps. The details differ, which is why the system is built around each business's own process. You can start without any software. Log how long your last ten quotes took, find the slowest step, and fix that first. If you want to go further, a custom system can remove manual steps entirely.
