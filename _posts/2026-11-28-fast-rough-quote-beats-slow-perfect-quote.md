Many manufacturers pride themselves on accuracy. They want every quote to be right, every cost covered, every detail checked. That instinct is good, and it comes from experience. But it can cost you jobs.

In a competitive market, a fast, reasonably accurate quote often beats a perfect one that arrives late. This article explains why, and how to get the balance right.

## The buyer's side of the story
Put yourself in the buyer's seat. You need a part. You send the drawing to three or four shops and wait. The first shop replies within a day with a clear price and lead time. The second replies in a week. The third never replies.

By the time the second quote arrives, you may have already made your decision. Or at least, the first price has set your expectations. A late quote, even a better one, has to overcome both the delay and the anchor.

This is not unfair. Buyers are busy. They reward the suppliers who make their jobs easier.

## Perfect is expensive
Chasing the perfect quote usually means waiting for every supplier price, checking every assumption, and having the owner review each line. Each step adds days. The gain in accuracy is often small, while the cost in lost opportunities is large.

That does not mean being sloppy. It means being appropriate. A repeat part you have made many times can be priced quickly and confidently. A genuinely new, complex job may need extra care. The skill is knowing which is which.

## The proof: 15 days to 1 day
An Australian HVAC reseller I worked with had quotes that took 15 days. We built a custom quoting system around the way they already worked, and quote time fell from 15 days to 1 day. The point is not the tool itself. The point is that the delay was in the process, not in the pricing. Once the process was fixed, the same business could reply in a fraction of the time.

That kind of change shifts the odds. A business that replies within a day is in a different position from one that replies in two weeks, even if the prices are identical.

## What "rough" really means
A fast quote is not a careless one. It is a quote built on good, ready information.
- Standard costs on file. You know your usual material and labour costs without hunting for them.
- Past jobs as a guide. Similar work you have done before gives you a reliable starting point.
- Clear assumptions. You state what is included, so the buyer knows what the price covers.
- Room to refine. If the details change, you update the quote.
The result is a number you can stand behind, delivered while the buyer is still listening.

## Questions to ask yourself
- How long does my quote really take, from enquiry to reply?
- How many jobs went to another shop before I replied?
- Where do the days go: waiting for information, waiting for people, or building from scratch?
- Which of my quotes could be priced within a day if the information were ready?

## Making speed safe
Owners worry that faster quoting means more mistakes. It does not have to. The safest way to quote quickly is to build the information the quote needs into one place: standard costs, past job results, and clear templates. The quote becomes an assembly job, not a research project.

It also helps to track what happened after the quote. If a fast quote turns out to be underpriced on a certain job type, you adjust. Over time, speed and accuracy improve together.

## What to do this week
Pick your five most common job types. For each, write down a typical price range, based on real past jobs. Use these as starting points to reply to the next five enquiries within a day, even if you add a note that the final price depends on confirmation of details.

You may find that buyers respond well to speed and clarity. You may also see which job types need more work before they can be priced quickly.

## A middle path for complex jobs
Not every job can be priced in a day. For a genuinely complex job, send a quick reply within a day that confirms you received the drawing, lists any questions, and gives a realistic date for the full quote. Buyers value being kept informed. It shows you are on top of the job and keeps you in the running while you do the detailed work.

## Frequently asked questions
**Will faster quotes mean more underpriced jobs?**
Not if you build them on real past data and check results afterwards. Compare quoted and actual hours on the jobs you win, and adjust the job types that run over.
